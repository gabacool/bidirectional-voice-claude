How the Fish TTS was made to work

Lessons learned, written 2026-10-20. Covers PR #24 (engine) and PR #26 (stall and volume fix) in the voice API on the Mac.

Fish Audio Drama 3 reads text aloud on the Mac at about 2x realtime (a typical figure, not a floor: 1.95x median in-process, slower when the Mac is short of memory). Getting it to read everything, at an even volume, with real pauses took six separate fixes. Each one started as something a person heard, became a number, and ended as a code change with a measurement behind it. This page keeps the reusable part: the method, the numbers, and the mistakes in our own measuring.

Not a spec. The design is in docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-fish-stall-and-volume-design.md. This page explains what went wrong, how we found out, and what to do differently next time.

Words used on this page

Piece = one sentence or clause fed to the model at a time. Example: "up 3.14 percent."
Chunk = one block of decoded audio, about 0.32 s. The first chunk of a request is smaller (4 frames).
Frame = one step of the audio decoder, about 46 ms (21.5 per second).
Codec = the audio decoder. It turns the model's codes into sound and keeps a short memory of the last sound it made.
Stall = the model goes silent for about 3 s in the middle of a piece instead of finishing it.
EOS (end of sequence) = the model says "this piece is done" on its own. Cutting it off early is not EOS.
RMS = average loudness of a block of audio. Normal speech chunks mostly sit above 0.01; 0.0004 or less is true silence.
STT (speech-to-text) = we transcribe the output and compare to the input text. It is our "did every word come out?" test.
Onset-steady = loudness 50-550 ms into a piece minus that piece's later loudness, in dB. +5 dB means the start is clearly louder.
dBFS = loudness against digital full scale; 0 is the loudest possible, so -30 is quieter than -27 (3 dB less).
SNR (signal-to-noise ratio, dB) = how closely two decodes of the same audio match. Higher is closer; 55 dB or more is a tiny difference.
p99 / p99.9 = the value that 99% (or 99.9%) of the measurements stay below. A limit set at p99 flags about 1 in 100.
RoPE table = the table of position codes the decoder reads as it advances; this one was built for 4,096 frames.
Watchdog = the engine code that watches the output for silence and decides a piece has stalled.
Prefix = the audio frames of a piece already played, handed back to the model so it continues rather than starts over.
R0-R3 = the rungs of the recovery ladder (diagram in 2.4): R1 retry from scratch, R2 resume, R3 repeat the piece, R0 skip.
Runaway = a piece that keeps producing sound far longer than its text needs (repeats or babble).

2. The problems, one at a time

2.1 The last word was cut off found 2026-10-17, PR #24

Symptom
Reads ended one word early. "...or the price action?" came out as "...or the price?".
Measurement
STT of the generated audio against the input text.
Root cause
The last word fades out in a quiet chunk. The silence watchdog counted that chunk as silence, held it like a pause, and dropped it when the piece ended.
Fix
Play the quiet fade-out tail instead of dropping it.
Evidence
STT before: "price?". After: "price action?". Later regression check on the final code: last word complete 8/8.

This same mistake (a silence line set too high) came back in problem 4 in a bigger form.

2.2 Pauses were near zero PR #24

Symptom
Sentences ran together. Commas had almost no gap.
Measurement
Silence runs in the output audio, compared with where punctuation sits in the text.
Root cause
Drama 3's own gap at a comma is about 0.1 s. The obvious remedy, an inline [pause] cue, makes the model stall. Paragraph breaks did worse: it stopped emitting EOS and sampled about 47 s of silence.
Fix

Never ask the model for a pause. Feed it one sentence or clause per piece, and insert an exact silence ourselves: 0.5 s after . ! ? ; and 0.25 s after , : (sentence_pause_seconds, clause_pause_seconds). Text is joined into one line first.

The splitter must not cut inside 1,255, 3.14, 10:30, Dr., U.S., an ellipsis, or before a closing quote. Fragments under 20 characters join the next piece.

Evidence
24-piece article: 23 of 23 boundaries had a pause, 11 at about 0.49 s and 12 at about 0.22 s. Later, 8 reads of a long text: sentence pause median 0.500 s (n=192), clause pause median 0.250 s (n=200). Speed 2.19x realtime vs 2.05-2.18x before. Content matched at 97% (STT) before and after.

2.3 A long read went silent tested 2026-10-17, PR #24

Symptom
Long text was not spoken to the end.
Measurement
A 190 s article fed as one piece (frame and chunk limits lifted), then the same article through the sentence/clause engine. STT recall and words per second over time.
Root cause
Drama 3 does not murmur on long text the way Vireo does (Vireo's trouble was its bilingual clone clip). Its failure is a silent stall: one long piece speaks clearly for about 53 s, then samples silence. This matches the library's own design of roughly 47 s or 300 bytes per piece.
Fix
Keep the sentence/clause splitting. It is required, not a nicety, and costs no measurable speed (about 2x realtime either way).
Evidence
One piece: 26% of the article read. Split: 190 s read at a steady 2.4-3.2 words per second, 97% STT recall.

Do not remove or lengthen splitting, and never use [pause] cues in the text.

2.4 Sentences were skipped seen in Origin chat, PR #26

Symptom
In voice chat, whole sentences or the tail of a sentence never played. Option+S market briefings with spelled-out tickers ("K L A C", "I R E N") stalled two or three times per read. The log showed 499 requests, 34 "skipping" events and 1 retry, but never the text.
Measurement

Per-chunk RMS histograms plus STT of every quiet stretch, on the real model.

  • Normal speech (1,093 chunks): 0 chunks below 0.001. Between 6% and 11% of chunks sit between 0.003 and 0.01.
  • True stall silence: 0.0004 or less. Natural word gaps: 0.0001-0.0003.
  • All 9 of 9 quiet runs (two or more chunks under 0.01) in a 173 s read were real words: "Fifty five.", "Opportunity.", "Technologies CX." (1.28 s), "October sixteenth." (1.28 s) and others.
  • Letting a stalled piece run on: it finished by itself in 4 of 5 long silences. One silence lasted 14 s, one never ended.
Root cause

(a) The silence line was too high. The watchdog treated any chunk under RMS 0.01 as silence, held it, cut it to 0.6 s and counted it toward the 3 s stall. Quiet sentence endings live at 0.003-0.01. Runs longer than 0.6 s lost words with no log line.

(b) Skip-on-stall threw away the rest of the piece. After a real stall the engine jumped to the next piece. In 3 of 3 aborted probe stalls, words were lost ("CRWV at sixty one point three nine." played, "down 11.9 percent," did not). The restart also began with a cold decoder.

(c) Triggers are random. Spelled tickers stalled 3 of 70 pieces vs 0 of 156 for other pieces, but nothing in the text predicts which one. One deterministic trigger: "I R E N. Worst cohort..." was one piece because a lone letter plus a period was read as an initial.

Fix
  1. Silence line to silence_rms: 0.001. Quiet words play in full and never count toward a stall.
  2. Resume in place instead of skip (ladder below). The model is re-prompted with the audio frames already played, so it continues instead of starting over.
  3. The resume prefix ends at the last frame with sound, not at the end of the last chunk. A chunk can end in silent frames; a prefix ending in silence made the model think the word was already said.
  4. A runaway budget per piece, in voiced frames, sized by how the text is spoken (letters, spelled letters, digits, symbols, CJK characters). Hitting it logs an ERROR and moves on without resuming.
  5. Every stall line carries the piece text. The splitter now ends a sentence after spelled letters ("S P Y.").
Evidence
CheckResult
Natural stalls in 556 chat requests (real model)27 events: 25 resumed, 2 retried, 0 skipped. STT recall min 0.952, mean 0.996 (508 requests checked).
Prefix A/B on 34 cuts, same snapshotPrefix to the last sounding frame: 0 trials lost words. Prefix plus 7 silent frames (what the engine passed first): 17 lost words, 19 ended at once, 3 ran to the 1,024-frame cap.
Forced resumes after the prefix fix59 forced resumes (60 trials, one did not stall), 0 lost words, 0 repeats.

2.5 Volume pumped on every chat chunk PR #26

Symptom
In voice chat each request (a sentence group) started louder, then settled lower. Per-second level over one chat request (dBFS): -26.9, -30.3, -29.3, -30.8, -30.2, -31.2, -30.9, -31.7.
Measurement

Onset-steady, split four ways. The same generated audio codes were decoded two ways, so only the decoder differed:

Text historyDecoderRequest-first pieceLater pieces
none (live)cold reset per request+6.43 dB+1.43
none (live)continuous across requests+1.49 dB+1.29
carried across requestscold reset per request+5.04 dB+1.11
carried across requestscontinuous+1.07 dB+1.31

The decoder start decides the onset. Text history does not. Option+S has one cold start per read (first piece +8.0 dB, n=1); chat has one per request (+5.2 dB, n=7); later pieces sit at +1.6 to +1.9 dB.

Root cause
Every request is a fresh model call, and a fresh call resets the decoder's streaming state. The first half second of a cold decoder is louder. Within one request the state carries over pieces, so only the first piece is hot. Origin sends one request per sentence group (the first sentence alone, then groups of a few sentences), so it gets this at the start of every request.
Fix

Keep the decoder state from the end of the last finished piece (a cheap snapshot, no array copies) and restore it at the start of the next request. The state is tagged with a signature of model, reference clip, and sampling settings, so a changed voice never inherits an old decoder. After a model or voice change, a warm-up of about 8 s of the voice's own speech seeds it. Config: carry_codec_state: true.

The decoder position can grow past the 4,096-frame table it was built for. We measured that: decoding after a 17,000-frame primer vs a 600-frame primer differs by SNR 55-67 dB and under 0.002 dB in level. Not audible.

Evidence
  • 70 paired requests, live vs the fix, same minute: onset-steady +5.03 dB to +0.85 dB.
  • Second 1 minus seconds 2-8 of each request: +3.73 dB to +0.84 dB (paired).
  • First audio within the gate threshold (median at most 0.25 s, p90 at most 0.35 s): median 0.212 s, p90 0.278 s over 415 streamed requests.

Rejected alternatives, and why

IdeaResult
Onset trim (hold the first 1.6 s of every piece)Adds about +0.7 s to first audio (0.81 s vs 0.15-0.35 s). Treats the symptom, and cuts the natural +1 dB sentence-start emphasis on every later piece. Rolled back.

2.6 Memory grew from 35 to 59 GB found during the gate, PR #26

Symptom
The voice process read 35 GB after load, 51 GB after about 20 requests and 59 GB after about 800. A private test copy sat 3-10 GB above the live service after the same traffic.
Measurement
Per-request counters inside the process (live arrays, cache, peak, process footprint) on the real model. Same request sequence, branch vs the old loop.
Root cause
Not a leak. Live arrays stayed flat at 24.17-24.23 GB. MLX keeps every freed GPU buffer for reuse and never gives it back by default. Drama 3 frees buffers of ever-new sizes (each piece's history sizes its caches), so the cache collects sizes it rarely reuses: 9.5 GB to 29.7 GB over 29 requests. The old code did the same (8.3 to 22.1 GB over 9 requests). Ruled out: carried decoder snapshots, history arrays, the voice cache.
Fix
Cap the allocator cache with mlx_cache_limit_gb: 4 (process-wide) and clear loader leftovers (8-9 GB) after the model loads. Chosen from paired runs: no cache costs about 10% speed and +0.05 s first audio; 4 GB was fastest to first audio.
Evidence
320 requests: active memory 24.173-24.232 GB, cache at most 4.075 GB, footprint 28.64-28.83 GB, first audio median 0.164 s. Uncapped, the same code reached 54.4 GB by request 29.

4. Lessons for next time

  1. Measure before fixing. Every fix here began with a probe: STT of the output plus per-chunk RMS. The silence line was found by listing the 9 quiet runs and reading what each said, not by guessing a number.
  2. Check the metric is fit for the question. A ruler that fails 64% of normal speech measures the ruler. Before trusting a pass or fail, run it on audio you know is fine and on audio you know is broken.
  3. Reproduce on the real model, not mocks. Fake-driver unit tests passed while the real model, given the prefix the engine first built, lost words in 17 of 34 resume trials. The implementer's own run saw 0 natural stalls in about 40 requests; the verifier's 556-request gate found 27. Rare failures need volume.
  4. Separate the two request paths when the symptom differs by path. Option+S pays one cold start per read, chat pays one per request. Test both, with Origin's real grouping (first sentence alone, then groups).
  5. Keep a rollback tag and a config-only mitigation. Tag voice-stable-live-20261019 marks the code before PR #26. Setting carry_codec_state: false, silence_rms: 0.01 and stall_attempts: 0 restores the old behaviour without touching code.
  6. Probes must stop their GPU processes. The Mac sat at 127 of 128 GB during the gate, so every latency number was taken under pressure, and one 16.8 s gap with no engine log line could not be explained. Run one extra model copy at a time, on its own port (9921), never the live service, and confirm the process table is clean afterwards.
  7. Make the log answer the next question. The old log recorded 34 skips but never which text. Every stall, retry, skip and runaway line now carries text='...' and the done line carries counters, so the next bug starts with evidence.
  8. Prefer recover to skip, and never lose text silently. Skipping is still allowed, but only after attempts are exhausted and only with an ERROR that names the text.
  9. Fix the cause, not the symptom. The onset trim would have hidden the loud start for +0.7 s of latency. Carrying the decoder removed the start at the source for no latency.
  10. Say what was not proven. See the next section.

5. Still open, and cost if wrong

  • Nobody has listened to a resume join, the voice change on a config reload, or a natural runaway by ear. The numbers say they are clean.
  • Real Origin chat turn (G15) was not run; the first real check is a person using chat after deploy.
  • The first 6 or so requests after a restart are weaker: onset-steady +1.90 dB pooled, +2.83 dB on chat-style text, against +0.79 dB once warm.
  • A full retry (rung R3) repeats words. It happened once in 27 natural stalls in the gate run, and 2 times in 7 in a later memory run.
  • A number-heavy piece that repeats once cannot be cut early without cutting normal takes (takes vary up to 1.45x). A natural runaway played about 6 s of repeated "one hundred forty two eighteen" before the cut.
  • Config reload only fires from /speak and /save. After editing the voice, the first chat request before the next Option+S pays the 4 s warm-up itself.

Cost if wrong: low and reversible. No data, money or auth is involved. Worst observed harms: one dropped word at a resume join (about 1 in 28 forced resumes, 0 in 22 natural, before the prefix fix), up to about 6 s of repeated numbers, and a louder start in the first few requests after a restart.

Undo: config-only mitigation above (hot reload), or check out tag voice-stable-live-20261019 and run launchctl kickstart -k "gui/$(id -u)/com.gabagool.voiceapi". The README has the full rollback steps.
